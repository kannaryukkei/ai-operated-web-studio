# Business Intelligence — ACFIX Studio (Simulated)

## Business model hypothesis

A local home-service provider offering routine split-AC cleaning, initial symptom inspection and new split-AC installation. The scenario excludes recurring maintenance contracts, central HVAC projects, parts retail and emergency response.

## Priority audience and jobs-to-be-done

| Hypothetical audience | Trigger | Core question | Website response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homeowner / family | AC seems less effective or overdue for cleaning | Is this a cleaning need or a fault? | Explain routine care vs inspection |
| Apartment resident / tenant | Dripping water or unusual operation | What can be checked before a visit? | Explain what details to prepare; no remote diagnosis |
| New unit buyer / household | Needs split AC installation | What affects installation estimate? | Explain that unit, route, materials and site condition affect scope |

These segments are proposed from the synthetic interview, not real customer research.

## Offer matrix

| Offer | Visitor intent | What is known in scenario | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routine cleaning | Maintain split AC | Hypothetical starting price Rp75,000/unit; refrigerant is not automatic | Exact cleaning steps, unit-size rules, time, exclusions |
| Initial inspection | Investigate weak cooling, leak or symptoms | Hypothetical starting price Rp50,000; physical inspection required | Whether fee is credited, scope and follow-on fees |
| Installation | Install new split AC | Hypothetical starting price Rp350,000 | Materials, piping/electrical work, brackets, unit size and site constraints |

## Customer decision friction

1. Confusing cleaning with repair.
2. Expecting chat diagnosis or guaranteed restored cooling.
3. Uncertainty about extra charges and installation materials.
4. Uncertainty about schedule and address-level coverage.
5. No public endpoint or approved proof to establish trust.

## Positioning hypothesis

“Jelas sebelum dikerjakan”: show service boundaries, explain what affects final scope, and require inspection/customer agreement before additional work. This is a communication hypothesis from the synthetic input, not a verified differentiator.

## Trust strategy

No social proof. Build clarity through process explanation, honest limits, transparent price caveats, preparation guidance and an explicit contact-unavailable state.

## Starter fit

One audience family and a single “understand the need → choose a service path → understand conditions → prepare for confirmation” journey. Static one-page site; no booking, persistent form, login, payment, remote diagnosis or integration.

## Hypotheses not promoted to facts

- A symptom selector may reduce confusion but is not required for this scope.
- Maintenance reminders may be useful later; no evidence and out of scope.
- Emergency service may have demand; it is not stated and must not be advertised.

# Buying Journey and Information Architecture

## Primary visitor job

“Help me understand what kind of AC service I might need, what affects the estimate, and what information to prepare before contacting a service provider.”

## Proposed one-page information architecture

1. Hero: simulated ACFIX identity + clear scope + prominent demo disclosure.
2. Choose the service path: routine cleaning / initial inspection / installation.
3. Price logic: fictional starting-price examples and adjacent conditions.
4. Know the boundary: cleaning does not establish all faults; refrigerant/repair is not automatic.
5. Process: explain → inspect → confirm scope/cost → obtain approval → work (scenario only).
6. Prepare for the visit: AC type, symptoms, unit position/access and installation context.
7. Coverage: broad hypothetical Surabaya/Sidoarjo only; exact address confirmation caveat.
8. FAQ: remote diagnosis, extra costs, schedule and policy unknowns.
9. Contact state: destination not configured; no form and not clickable.

No testimonials, ratings, credentials, counters, technician profiles, maps, booking UI, lead form, guarantees or service-area district lists.

Each section directly answers a recorded visitor question. Page remains compact because it resolves a narrow service-choice task, not because a target section count is required.

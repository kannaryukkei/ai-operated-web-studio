# Client Material Ingestion

## Purpose

Understand the business from the smallest practical input. The normal input is a conversation/interview, transcript or notes, existing assets and optional source links. Do not make the client complete a long questionnaire or write website copy.

The project conversation is the working context. Create a brief, fact list, asset register or other file only when it materially improves clarity, verification, delivery or handover.

## Select the operating mode first

- `client_service` is the default for real or ambiguous work. Use real source material. Do not invent client facts, claims, prices, policies, permissions, contacts or social proof.
- `studio_development` allows clearly labelled synthetic businesses, facts and test assets for internal development. Synthetic information is not real evidence and cannot be used for public release.
- Keep project identities and source materials separate. Never transfer synthetic information into client service without replacing or independently verifying it.

## Inputs

- Interview recording, transcript, notes or a conversational summary.
- Logo, brand guide, product/service photos, videos, catalogues, documents, policies, portfolio or other materials the business supplies.
- Optional current website, public profiles, public contact destinations and reference websites.
- Earlier confirmed decisions for the same project, kept distinct from new source evidence.

Proceed with available material when a source type is missing. Missing photography or a brand guide is not automatically a blocker.

## Workflow

### 1. Preserve and inspect

- Keep original files unchanged when working with supplied materials.
- Inventory the files and links that materially inform the website. Note source, owner/provider if known, date and access location.
- Exclude or quarantine credentials, private customer records, personal IDs and other unnecessary sensitive data.
- Never claim a platform or ingestion tool performed work that was actually done manually.

### 2. Transcribe and locate

- Use available transcription capability when needed; otherwise use a supplied transcript or notes.
- Keep original recordings separate from derived transcripts.
- For material claims, retain a useful locator: timestamp, page/paragraph, image/file, URL section or a short source excerpt.
- Mark unclear speech and uncertain values rather than silently resolving them.

### 3. Extract facts and uncertainty

Extract one material claim at a time. For each important claim, keep:

- What was said or observed.
- The supporting source and locator.
- Whether it is provided, not available, not applicable, uncertain or conflicting.
- Whether evidence independently verifies it or it is only client-provided.
- Whether it may be published, is pending or is not approved.
- Any material conflict and the person/action needed to resolve it.

A client statement can be provided, unverified and pending publication at the same time. Do not collapse these into one status.

In `studio_development`, label synthetic records as `SYNTHETIC / DEVELOPMENT ONLY`. A simulated review, metric, price, address or outcome is a test fixture, not a business fact.

### 4. Inspect supplied assets

For each asset that may be used, identify its source/owner where known, intended use, visible subject, relevance, format/dimensions, crop/focal point and any important restrictions. For real client work, confirm commercial-use/publication permission and people/property consent when applicable. Client-supplied does not automatically mean client-owned or licensed.

Never infer credentials, product specifications, ingredients, service quality, people's identities/consent, authenticity of testimonials or publication rights from pixels, metadata or filenames alone. Keep unclear rights pending and out of public use.

### 5. Resolve only material gaps

Do not ask for information already present in the conversation or sources. Ask a concise question only when the answer materially changes the offer, scope, price, customer journey, public claim, CTA, asset use, legal/safety policy or release.

Classify unresolved items by practical effect:

- **Preview blocker:** a safe, coherent preview cannot be made even with a disclosed omission or unavailable state.
- **Production blocker:** preview can continue, but public release must wait (for example: current price, public destination, material policy, asset rights or consent).
- **Optional gap:** it does not affect the agreed journey now; omit the unsupported claim or feature and do not ask merely to fill a record.

Continue unaffected work when safe. An unanswered item remains unresolved; do not repeatedly ask unless the next decision depends on it.

### 6. Prepare the working handoff

Summarize in the project conversation:

- Business model, audience, offers and how customers buy.
- Confirmed facts, source support and material conflicts.
- Existing assets and any rights/permission questions.
- Customer questions, buying journey and intended website action.
- Essential content gaps and safe omissions.
- Only the clarifications that materially block the next decision.

For complex or regulated projects, create a concise source/fact or asset/permission file when the volume justifies it. Do not create a fixed set of 12 intake artifacts for every website.

## Quality check

Before design or implementation, confirm that:

- All supplied material relevant to the current scope was reviewed.
- Public claims can be supported by sources or are clearly identified as missing/assumptions.
- Conflicting facts remain visible until resolved.
- Rights, consent and public-use permissions are not inferred.
- Private project contacts are not treated as public CTA destinations.
- The selected operating mode is explicit and synthetic information cannot be mistaken for real evidence.
- The client is not being asked to repeat information or complete an unnecessary technical form.

## Failure handling

- Unclear transcription: preserve the uncertain passage and ask only if material.
- Unreadable asset: record the limitation; request a usable file only if needed.
- Unknown rights: do not publish the asset; choose a verified alternative or clearly disclosed concept.
- Conflicting facts: keep the claim out of approved copy until resolved.
- Incomplete sources: continue with supported content and disclose material gaps.

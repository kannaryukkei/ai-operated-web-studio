# Independent Visual Design Audit

Act as the lead designer responsible for the quality of the deliverable. The owner is the final approver, not the primary defect detector. Complete this audit before asking the owner for general visual feedback.

## Inputs

Read the approved Client Brief, design blueprint, design direction/tokens, copy deck, asset register and implemented source. Capture actual desktop, tablet and mobile screenshots. If a live browser or screenshot cannot be accessed, identify the limitation and do not claim a visual review.

## Audit every segment

Review all relevant page segments, including:
1. Announcement, header and navigation.
2. Hero and primary action.
3. Every primary content/discovery section.
4. Offer, product, service, project or proof sections.
5. Supporting guidance, FAQs, policy or process sections.
6. Contact/conversion or unavailable-state area.
7. Footer and legal/credit information.

For each segment record:
- Purpose and visitor question.
- What works and should be preserved.
- Visual issues and their evidence.
- Severity and effect on comprehension, trust, usability or brand perception.
- Concrete correction.
- Final status: FIXED, DEFERRED or RETAINED WITH REASON.

## Cross-page design critique

Evaluate independently:
- Distinctiveness and fit to the subject/brand.
- Visual hierarchy and section rhythm.
- Layout composition, grid, alignment, whitespace and content density.
- Typography roles, scale, line length and readability.
- Palette, contrast and restrained use of accents.
- Image choice, image quality, focal point, aspect ratio and crop.
- Consistency of cards, buttons, borders, icons and interaction states.
- Responsive adaptation and reading order.
- Motion, focus and keyboard-visible states.

## Screenshot review method

- Review desktop, tablet and mobile at actual viewport scale (100%).
- Use full-page screenshots to evaluate section rhythm, not as sole evidence of text legibility.
- Inspect important sections in viewport-sized screenshots.
- Check at least one long-content state and one interactive state when applicable.
- Compare the rendered result against the approved blueprint and real source content.
- Do not invent visual test results or assume the screenshot represents an untested viewport.

## Cross-project distinctiveness (required)

A visually clean and usable page can still be generic. Judge the design as a creative deliverable, not only as a collection of defects.

When the project repository or design archive contains prior work:
- Inspect the design fingerprints and rendered references of at least the two most recent relevant projects. If the original repository or screenshot is unavailable, state that limitation rather than inventing a comparison.
- Compare macro-composition, hero treatment, section sequence, content grid, typographic voice, image language, surface system and interaction grammar.
- Record which patterns are reused, which are meaningfully new, and why any intentional reuse fits this client's visitor task.
- Check common anti-patterns: repeated all-caps labels, eyebrows above every heading, arbitrary numbers/counters, a default split hero, identical card grids, decorative arrows and generic section sequences. Do not ban a pattern automatically; require a clear business or usability reason for retaining it.
- A palette, font pairing or illustration change alone is not sufficient evidence of distinctiveness.

### Required aesthetic verdict

Return exactly one:
- **DISTINCTIVE** — composition and visual grammar clearly fit this client and are not mistaken for a re-skin of prior work.
- **COMPETENT BUT GENERIC** — coherent and usable, but too dependent on familiar template structure or decoration.
- **DERIVATIVE** — primarily a re-skin or near-copy of earlier work.

Only DISTINCTIVE is a default pass for owner visual review. COMPETENT BUT GENERIC and DERIVATIVE require focused redesign before owner review, unless the owner explicitly accepts a documented exception. "No blocker found" is not an aesthetic verdict and cannot replace it.

## Prioritization

Fix in this order:
1. Factual, brand or conversion integrity.
2. Comprehension and readability.
3. Hierarchy, navigation and interaction clarity.
4. Responsive layout and asset framing.
5. Composition, spacing and consistency.
6. Decorative polish.

Prefer removing or simplifying weak elements over adding more sections, cards, animations or visual effects. Preserve the strongest parts of the design. Do not redesign the brand without a reason in the brief or evidence from the audit.

## Output

Return:
- Overall design verdict.
- Segment-by-segment audit table.
- Cross-page system findings.
- Ranked fixes with specific implementation changes.
- Screenshots reviewed and viewport dimensions.
- Remaining subjective decisions requiring owner approval.
- Explicit NOT TESTED areas.

Do not ask the owner to identify obvious defects that can be detected from the source or rendered output.

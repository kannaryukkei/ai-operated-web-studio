# Hostinger Good Website Benchmark v1.0

**Purpose:** Translate the supplied Hostinger article into a practical, evidence-based review reference for AI-Operated Web Studio website projects.  
**Source basis:** Owner-supplied `What Makes a Good Website.docx` (23 pages), including the sections **Factors to Consider** and **15 Qualities that Make a Good Website**.  
**Live source:** [What Makes a Good Website? 15 Characteristics To Stand Out (From Design to UX)](https://www.hostinger.com/tutorials/what-makes-a-good-website/)  
**Article page metadata:** The live canonical page displayed “Last updated Mar 30, 2023” when checked on 2026-11-20.  
**Review date:** 2026-11-20.

## 1. How to use this benchmark

This document preserves the source article's two-layer structure:

1. **Factors to Consider:** Design, Functionality, Features, SEO and User Experience.
2. **15 Qualities:** A more specific list of website characteristics.

The first layer guides the design and product decisions. The second layer is a cross-check for a broad website review or acceptance audit.

This is a practical benchmark, not a universal feature checklist or a certification. Apply each point according to the actual business, audience, website type, approved scope and available evidence. For example, checkout is central to an e-commerce site but not applicable to a simple service brochure; breadcrumbs and internal search may be unnecessary on a well-structured one-page site.

Do not calculate a single numeric “website quality score” by treating all 15 characteristics as equally important. Record a separate status and evidence for each relevant characteristic. A criterion that is not relevant is **N/A**, not a failure; a criterion that was not checked is **NOT TESTED**, not a pass.

The benchmark does not override the Studio's existing requirements for factual integrity, rights and licensing, privacy, security, truthful interactions, accessibility, visual quality or release authorization.

## 2. Factors to Consider

### Design

The source article highlights design as a first impression and as a determinant of how visitors interact with website content. Its practical design considerations are:

- **Engaging color schemes:** choose a coherent palette that supports the brand identity and intended emotional tone.
- **Appropriate fonts:** choose type that remains compatible and readable across devices; use appropriate sizes and spacing.
- **Clear visual hierarchy:** use proportion, contrast, grids and whitespace to help visitors identify important information. Keep typography and layout consistent.
- **Web accessibility:** provide useful image alternative text, logical heading structure, captions for meaningful audio/video and accessibility testing.

**AWOS interpretation:** judge the rendered design by business fit, hierarchy, legibility, imagery, accessibility and responsive behavior. A different palette alone is not meaningful design differentiation.

### Functionality

The source distinguishes website purpose by business type. An e-commerce website supports shopping; a business website presents the business and its offers professionally and helps generate sales or revenue.

Before selecting features, identify the website's purpose, target audience and what content or functionality those visitors need.

**AWOS interpretation:** only implement functionality that supports the real visitor journey. Do not add booking, checkout, lead forms, search, accounts, analytics or other systems merely because they appear on a generic quality checklist.

### Features

The source lists these common features:

- A clear, attention-grabbing headline that explains the website.
- A hero image or video that communicates the website's message or theme.
- Prominent, clearly labelled navigation.
- Visible call-to-action buttons.
- Social proof such as testimonials or partner badges.
- Clear, concise and consistent content.
- Images and video that add information and help explain the offer.

**AWOS interpretation:** social proof must be genuine and authorized. Images and video must be relevant, rights-cleared and accurately represented. Every CTA must lead to a real, approved action.

### SEO

The source discusses keyword research, technical optimization, backlinks and content optimization. It specifically mentions fast loading, mobile-friendliness, freedom from broken links, analytics configuration, semantic content and well-structured headings.

**AWOS interpretation:** check whether the site can be crawled and indexed as intended, whether titles/headings/content describe the real offer, whether important links work and whether performance has actually been measured. Backlink acquisition and analytics strategy may be outside the scope of an individual website build. Do not claim search ranking or SEO performance without suitable evidence.

### User Experience

The source frames UX around understanding users' goals, pain points and behavior, then making the journey intuitive. It emphasizes visuals, concise copy and logical navigation. Its product-search, recommendation and checkout example is specifically relevant to e-commerce.

**AWOS interpretation:** check whether visitors can understand the offer, judge whether it fits their needs, trust the information and complete the intended next step without unnecessary friction.

## 3. The 15 website qualities

| # | Source characteristic | AWOS review and evidence |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Easy to Navigate** | Check that the header and navigation labels describe the destination; the page hierarchy and reading order are understandable; footer links include relevant contact/legal destinations; and all links work. Use breadcrumbs for genuine multi-level hierarchy, dropdowns for meaningful sub-sections and a sitemap/structure overview for larger sites. Add internal search only when the amount of content justifies it. These elements may be unnecessary on a one-page site. Check native browser back/forward behavior. |
| 2 | **Good Page Speed** | Measure the real site with an appropriate tool such as PageSpeed Insights or GTmetrix. Inspect image formats, dimensions and compression; code minification/compression; caching and CDN use where appropriate. Record actual measurements and test conditions. Do not report “fast” or Core Web Vitals as verified when they were not measured. |
| 3 | **Responsive Design** | Verify that layout and interactions adapt to the intended phone, tablet and desktop widths. Inspect wrapping, overflow, navigation, image crops, reading order, touch targets and sticky/fixed elements in a real browser. A build passing is not proof of responsive quality. |
| 4 | **Follow Security Protocols** | Verify HTTPS/SSL for the deployed site when applicable; review dependency and framework updates; inspect security risks relevant to the stack and functionality; and keep credentials/secrets out of client-side code and repositories. Password-strength checks apply when the site actually has accounts or administration. Use security scans when appropriate and report what they cover. |
| 5 | **Optimized for Search Engines** | Check indexability settings, page titles and descriptions, meaningful headings, semantic page structure, relevant content, broken links and mobile/performance fundamentals. Verify that keywords reflect real visitor intent and the actual offer. Apply backlink work only when it is in scope. |
| 6 | **Structured Data** | Add structured data only when a supported schema type accurately describes real, visible and source-backed content. Validate the markup. Never invent ratings, reviews, prices, addresses, availability or other facts to populate rich results. |
| 7 | **Simple Inquiry and Checkout Process** | For e-commerce, reduce unnecessary checkout steps/fields; make price, shipping and delivery information clear; explain each step; secure the transaction; and make support accessible. For service websites, review the equivalent inquiry/booking journey. The CTA must not claim that a request is sent, stored or booked unless that actually happens. Mark checkout N/A for non-commerce sites. |
| 8 | **Up to Date Content** | Check that services, products, pricing, policies, locations, contact destinations and other time-sensitive facts are current and supported. Keep frameworks, dependencies and platform software maintained. Do not create a blog or update cadence without a real business need. |
| 9 | **Clear Value Proposition** | Confirm that the intended audience can quickly understand what is offered, the benefit, the problem solved and why the offer is relevant or distinct. Check this in the first screen and the sections that explain the offer. Differentiators must be supported by evidence, not invented. |
| 10 | **Good Copy** | Check that language reflects the audience's intent and questions, addresses real needs, stays clear and concise, avoids unnecessary jargon and errors, and supports the intended action. Persuasive language must remain truthful. A/B testing is meaningful only when variants, real traffic and valid measurement exist. |
| 11 | **Relevant Content** | Confirm that the site answers the questions visitors need answered to understand, evaluate and act. Use useful product/service details, explanations, FAQs, guides or other formats where relevant. Blogs, infographics, eBooks and webinars are optional content strategies, not mandatory website sections. |
| 12 | **Clear CTAs** | Check that each important CTA is visually distinct from surrounding content, action-oriented, concise and clear about the benefit or next step. Its destination must be real and approved. Do not add false urgency, countdowns, fake availability or buttons that do nothing. |
| 13 | **Quality Visuals** | Inspect image/video relevance, originality, quality, composition, crop, responsive behavior and alignment with the business identity. Prefer authentic client material when permission allows. The article favors original visuals over generic stock; AWOS applies its separate media-rights policy, which permits properly licensed stock as supporting context when appropriate, never as false client proof. |
| 14 | **Maintain Site Engagement** | Use real behavioral evidence to understand how visitors use the site and where improvements may help. Analytics or tracking must have an approved purpose and respect privacy, consent and applicable requirements. Do not install tracking by default or claim engagement gains without data. |
| 15 | **Capture Quality Leads** | Check that the site provides a suitable contact or lead path for the business and that submitted information has a real, secure destination and follow-up process. Segmentation is useful only when the business can act on it and the collection is disclosed and justified. Do not add nonfunctional lead forms or invent a CRM workflow. |

## 4. Review status and evidence

Use these statuses for each applicable characteristic:

- **PASS** — the criterion is satisfied in the tested scope and evidence supports the result.
- **CONCERN** — partially satisfied, weak, inconsistent or not fully substantiated.
- **FAIL** — a material requirement is broken, misleading or absent.
- **N/A** — genuinely not relevant to this website or its approved scope.
- **NOT TESTED** — no sufficient verification was performed.

For each concern or failure, record the relevant page/section, viewport or user state, evidence, visitor/business impact and required correction. Use the existing AWOS severity levels (BLOCKER, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW, NIT) rather than inventing a new severity scale.

Evidence can include rendered screenshots, measured performance output, link/interaction tests, source-backed content review, structured-data validation, security/dependency checks and privacy review. State exact widths, browser/environment and test method where relevant. A screenshot does not prove performance or accessibility; a successful build does not prove visual quality; an analytics setup does not prove increased engagement or leads.

## 5. Fit with AWOS review

Use this reference as a cross-check during a broad website review, not as an additional mandatory project stage or artifact package.

- **AESTHETIC:** primarily informs design, hierarchy, value proposition, copy, relevant content, CTA visibility and visual quality.
- **INTERFACE:** primarily informs navigation, responsiveness, accessibility, inquiry/checkout behavior and interaction clarity.
- **ACCEPTANCE:** checks the applicable 15 qualities against agreed scope and actual evidence, including performance, SEO, security, structured data, content freshness, analytics and lead handling where relevant.
- **VISUAL_FIDELITY:** remains a comparison against an approved visual reference; the Hostinger list does not replace that reference.

The review must prioritize the actual business objective and critical visitor journey. Do not let conditional features or generic best practices create needless complexity. The owner is asked only when a genuine fact, permission, scope, commercial decision or consequential approval is missing.
